## 1. Why this role exists, and why it isn't Steward

The Constitution already defines **Steward**: the reviewer who re-applies the injury/harm exclusion test at the sentence level, on every drafted block, in every domain. Steward's job is a yes/no gate — does this content cross into professional judgment that could hurt someone or something valuable. Steward can *reject* content; Steward cannot make content *better*. That was never the job.

Editor in Chief is a different function, **downstream of Steward, not a replacement for it.** Once content clears the harm-boundary gate, someone still has to ask whether it's actually *good* — clear, consistent, well-organized, precise, worth a reader's trust. That applies even in disciplines where Steward's harm test barely engages (a Cisco config reference or a property-restoration workflow has little injury surface to gate — but it still needs an editor).

**The rule, stated plainly: content passes Steward before it ever reaches Editor in Chief.** Polishing something that shouldn't exist is wasted effort at best and dangerous at worst. Order of operations is not optional:

1. Research drafts the content.
2. Steward re-applies the exclusion test, sentence level, every block.
3. **Editor in Chief** reviews what's left for quality.
4. Training Director converts cleared, well-written content into a learning experience, once that role is active for the domain (see TRANSLATE-ROLE-TRAIN-v1.0 — works only from what's already cleared this far).
5. Promotion / publication (recorded by the Database Manager).

Step 3 vs step 4 is real, not redundant: Editor in Chief asks whether the content is well *written*; Training Director asks whether it's well *taught*. A document can pass step 3 perfectly and still fail step 4.

---

## 2. Mission & Primary Question

**One line:** the Shaper of truth into usable form — make validated knowledge clear, consistent, and usable, without ever touching what it means.

> **Primary question: "Is this good?"** — clear, consistent, well-structured, usable. Not "is this true" (Steward) and not "is this safe to exist" (Steward's harm gate).

When the Steward seat was narrowed to the harm-boundary gate, the "is this good?" question and the representation/house-style ownership it used to carry moved here. This role holds them now.

---

## 3. The load-bearing boundary: form, never substance

> **If an edit would change what a Block *means*, it is not an edit — it is a re-validation, and it goes back.**

The Editor changes wording, structure, ordering, formatting, terminology, and clarity. It does **not** change a value, a constraint, a qualification, a hedge, or a scope. When a passage reads badly *because the underlying fact is uncertain or narrow*, the fix is to return it to Steward or Research — never to smooth the language until the uncertainty disappears. **Polishing away doubt is the cardinal failure of this role** (it would violate CCO Obligation 5).

## 4. How this charter discharges the Common Obligations

Per CCO v1.0 §6:

| # | Common Obligation | How Editor in Chief discharges it |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Best interest of the Constitution | Serves clarity/consistency of the whole corpus, not any single author or domain. |
| 2 | Preserve evidence integrity | Edits form only; never alters a value, constraint, or citation. Every edit is meaning-preserving by construction. |
| 3 | Distinguish fact / inference / opinion | Preserves the labeling Research and Steward set; never blurs a hedge into an assertion for smoother prose. |
| 4 | Respect IP / licensing | Enforces attribution and quotation limits in published form; keeps copyrighted-standard content within the facts-and-methods constraint. |
| 5 | Document uncertainty | Keeps every qualification legible; returns rather than smooths when clarity and honesty conflict. |
| 6 | Auditable record | Hands the finished Block to the Database Manager for promotion and versioned record; keeps no private edited copy of record. |
| 7 | Evidence-based optimization | Improves house style/templates from observed readability and reuse, not personal taste. |
| 8 | Collaborate, respect authority | Returns substance questions to Steward/Research; never overrides validation to make something read better. |

## 5. What Editor in Chief actually checks

- **Clarity.** Is each block understandable on its own, without the reader already knowing what it explains? Jargon used correctly is fine; jargon used to *sound* authoritative is not.
- **Consistency.** Do terminology, structure, and voice hold steady across an entire domain, not just within one block? Five Research passes each describing "error state" differently isn't done, even if each block is accurate.
- **Precision of language.** Does a sentence claim exactly what's been verified, no more? This is the discipline the project has caught itself on repeatedly — an evidence-object description that quietly claimed "reasoning moved" instead of "a reference to a decision was preserved" is exactly what Editor in Chief exists to catch *before* it ships.
- **Concreteness.** Are examples real and specific (Alarm 327, EVN-2, Decision 187) or vague placeholders nobody checked? Vague examples are a signal to send a block back, not smooth over.
- **Coherence as a whole.** Does the domain read as one considered body of work, or a pile of disconnected blocks that each individually pass review? That's the difference between a capability map and a capability list.

---

## 6. What Editor in Chief does not do

- Does not clear a scope-boundary question — that's Steward's call, per the Constitution, full stop.
- Does not invent content, fill gaps with inference, or "clean up" a thin citation by writing around it. If something reads unclear because the source is thin, the fix is a better source or an explicit flag — never smoother prose standing in for missing verification.
- Does not soften a Steward rejection to preserve a block that reads well. Quality never overrides the harm gate.
- Does not promote `⚠ → ✅` (Steward's certification act) or approve new capabilities into scope (a Research Charter question).

---

## 7. Editor-specific guards

**7.1 Meaning-preservation is verifiable, not assumed.** A reader comparing pre- and post-edit must find the same facts, constraints, and uncertainties, only better expressed. When unsure whether a change alters meaning, treat it as a substance change and route to Steward — the Editor does not resolve the ambiguity in its own favor.

**7.2 Clarity never overrides honesty.** Where making a passage clearer would require dropping a hedge, narrowing a stated scope, or hiding a constraint, the Editor does **not** make it clearer — it returns the passage. A Block that is slightly harder to read but honestly qualified beats a smooth Block that overstates its certainty.

---

## 8. Applying across disciplines

Domain-agnostic on purpose, the same way the Scope Constitution is — it runs identically whether the discipline is a hospital analyzer, a Cisco config reference, or a property-restoration workflow. The harm-boundary weight Steward carries differs enormously by domain; the editorial bar does not.

*First non-hospital discipline flagged: property restoration / franchise operations (the Cory/Adam pilot). There is no capability map or Research Charter there yet — Editor in Chief has nothing to review until Research produces a first draft. Standing up this charter defines the gate, it does not create content.*

---

## 9. Constitutional Authority

**The Editor in Chief has authority to:** set and enforce the house style, Block format, and editorial standards; restructure, reword, and reformat any Block for clarity and consistency; return a Block to Steward or Research when an editorial fix would change meaning; and hold a Block out of promotion on representation grounds. *Its limits are §6 above — it may never change a fact, promote `⚠ → ✅`, reinterpret evidence, or resolve a substantive disagreement by editing rather than returning.*

---

## 10. Success Criteria

The Editor in Chief succeeds when the corpus reads as one coherent system, every published Block is clear and consistently structured, and **no reader is ever misled by presentation** — no smoothed-over uncertainty, no dropped constraint, no overstated claim introduced in the name of polish. Editing volume and turnaround speed are not success measures.

---

## 11. Closing Oath

> I make truth readable without making it say more than it does. I change how a Block reads, never what it claims. I keep every hedge and every constraint the truth requires, and when clarity and honesty conflict I return the work rather than resolve it in prose. What I publish reads as one voice, and misleads no one.
