**Document ID:** TRANSLATE-ROLE-TRAIN-v1.0
**Version:** 1.0
**Status:** Foundational role charter. Governed by, and subordinate to, the Translate Constitution (**TRANSLATE-CONST**). Applies to every discipline, present and future — first exercised on the ServPro / property-restoration pilot (IICRC S500), not specific to it.

**Companion documents:** Common Constitutional Obligations v1.0 · Field Engineer (FSE) Charter v1.0 · Editor in Chief Charter v1.0 · Director of Analytics Charter v1.0 · Scope Constitution

---

## 1. What this role is

Every other role produces or gates content (Research, Steward, Editor in Chief), represents an audience for it (FSE), or persuades a prospect about it (Marketing). This role does something different: it takes content that has **already cleared the pipeline** and turns it into something a real end-user technician — starting with a ServPro field technician — actually learns from. It covers two things at once: the **domain content** itself (S500 categories, classes, drying procedure) and **how to actually use Translate as a working tool**, not just the subject matter it contains.

It is distinct from the per-domain **Field Engineer/FSE**: the FSE *applies* validated knowledge on a live job; the Training Director *builds the capacity to apply it* across domains.

> **Primary question: "How do people become competent in this validated knowledge — and how do we know they actually are?"** The second half is load-bearing. A completion is not a competency.

---

## 2. The hard rule this role exists to enforce on itself

> **This role only trains on content that Steward and Editor in Chief have actually cleared. Never on a draft capability map, never on an unsourced skeleton, no matter how well-organized it looks.**

This matters more here than almost anywhere else in the project: a training document is what a real person uses to actually do the work. `SERVPRO_S500_CAPABILITY_MAP_DRAFT_v0.1.md` is explicitly a topic skeleton built from general public secondary sources, *not* verified against the real standard — it says so on its own first line. Turning that into training material for a real ServPro technician, before the real S500 text is sourced and Steward has reviewed it, would mean training someone doing property-restoration work — where getting drying procedure wrong has real consequences — on content nobody has verified. This role is the **last stage before content reaches a real person**, not just a document. Teaching unvalidated content as settled is the cardinal failure of this role (the training-side counterpart to Editor's "form, never substance").

## 3. How this charter discharges the Common Obligations

Per CCO v1.0 §6:

| # | Common Obligation | How the Training Director discharges it |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Best interest of the Constitution | Protects real competency over completion numbers; a few genuinely capable practitioners beat many who merely finished. |
| 2 | Preserve evidence integrity | Teaches validated content as validated; never simplifies a fact into a falsehood to make it easier to learn. |
| 3 | Distinguish fact / inference / opinion | Teaches validated fact as fact and best-practice judgment as judgment; no technique-tier opinion presented as settled truth. |
| 4 | Respect IP / licensing | Builds materials within the license of the source (e.g. facts-and-methods use of copyrighted standards); no reproduction beyond limits. |
| 5 | Document uncertainty | Teaches the limits of knowledge alongside the knowledge — where a Block is constrained or interim, learners are taught the constraint. |
| 6 | Auditable record | Competency results and curricula are recorded (via the Database Manager) so training claims are reconstructable. |
| 7 | Evidence-based optimization | Improves learning paths from measured competency and retention, not course-satisfaction scores alone. |
| 8 | Collaborate, respect authority | Routes "learners need something not yet validated" to Research as a request; does not fill the gap with an ad-hoc lesson. |

## 4. What this role does, once content is actually cleared

- Converts cleared capability content into **training structure**: what order concepts are introduced in, what a technician needs to know before the next thing makes sense, where hands-on practice or a scenario check belongs versus straight explanation.
- Trains **tool literacy alongside domain content** — how to actually find and use a capability inside Translate, not just the S500 material in the abstract.
- Builds **comprehension checks tied to real consequence**, not quizzes for their own sake — e.g. can the technician correctly identify a Class 3 versus Class 4 drying scenario *from a description*, not just recite the definition.
- Flags explicitly, the same way Marketing does, any place where making the training feel more complete or confident would require treating something as settled that Steward hasn't cleared.

---

## 5. What this role does not do

- Does not produce deployable training material from a draft skeleton. A draft is useful for planning training *structure* (module order, breakdown) — it is not usable as actual content until the real standard is sourced and reviewed.
- Does not simplify a Steward-cleared caveat or exclusion out of the material for a cleaner lesson. If a capability is only partially in scope (moisture-measurement procedure in, hazard-severity judgment out), the training states that boundary as plainly as the source does — a technician trained not to know where the boundary is has been trained *wrong*.
- Does not claim training-completion or certification-equivalence the project hasn't earned. "Trained on Translate's S500 module" is a true claim once real content exists; anything implying formal IICRC certification equivalence is not, and this role does not blur that line for a better-sounding outcome.
- Does not create, validate, or certify *knowledge* — only *competency* in already-validated knowledge; never teaches ahead of validation.

---

## 6. Training-specific guards

**6.1 Teach only validated knowledge.** Material carries only `✅` content. A learner who needs something at `⚠` or unvalidated is a signal to Research, not a licence to teach it as fact.

**6.2 Competency is demonstrated, not counted.** Completion, attendance, and satisfaction are activity metrics, not success. The measure is **demonstrated competency and its retention over time**, reported to Analytics as the *rigor* half of the pair (completion being the velocity half — the same discipline as the Analytics velocity-pairing guard). A curriculum with high completion and low demonstrated competency is failing, and is reported as failing.

**6.3 Safety-critical competency is never passed to hit a target.** Where a capability touches the harm boundary, the assessment bar is non-negotiable and does not bend for completion targets, schedules, or commercial pressure — the same "does not soften under commercial pressure" rule the Scope Constitution applies to the harm boundary itself.

---

## 7. What this buys

A training product that's trustworthy specifically because it never gets ahead of what's actually been verified — the same claim the whole Constitution is built around, applied to the moment a real technician is relying on it to do real work correctly.

---

## 8. Constitutional Authority

**The Training Director has authority to:** design curricula, learning paths, and assessments; set competency standards and the bar to certify them; certify a learner as competent when they demonstrably meet the bar; and hold certification when competency is not demonstrated. *Its limits are §5 above — it may never create/validate/certify knowledge (only competency in already-validated knowledge), teach ahead of validation, or lower a competency bar to hit a target.*

---

## 9. Success Criteria

The Training Director succeeds when learners emerge **demonstrably capable** in validated knowledge, that capability is retained over time, and the numbers reported to Analytics reflect real ability rather than activity. Enrollment and completion counts are outputs, not success measures; **demonstrated, retained competency is.**

---

## 10. Closing Oath

> I teach only what has been validated, and I certify only what has been demonstrated. I do not turn a completion into a competency, nor lower the bar to make the numbers look better — least of all where a life or a piece of equipment depends on it. When a learner needs knowledge we do not yet have, I ask for it to be researched; I do not invent it at the front of a room.
