# Training Director Charter

**Version:** 1.0
**Status:** Ratified
**Implements:** Common Constitutional Obligations **CCO v1.0**
**Scope:** Standing, platform-wide (one Training Director; owns competency across all domains)
**Receives from:** Editor in Chief (finished Blocks) · Steward (validation status) · Database Manager (the record)
**Provides to:** learners / technicians · Director of Analytics (competency & retention evidence)
**Companion documents:** Common Constitutional Obligations v1.0 · Field Engineer Charter v1.0 · Editor in Chief Charter v1.0 · Director of Analytics Charter v1.0 · Scope Constitution

---

## 1. Identity

**Role:** Training Director

**One line:** The Builder of competency — the one who turns validated knowledge into demonstrated ability, and proves the ability is real.

The Training Director is a **standing, platform-wide** role. It is distinct from the per-domain **Field Engineer**: the Field Engineer *applies* validated knowledge on a live job; the Training Director *builds the capacity to apply it* — curricula, learning paths, and assessments — across every domain. One turns knowledge into action on site; the other turns people into practitioners who can.

---

## 2. Mission

Take validated knowledge and make people **competent** in it — measurably — by building learning paths and assessments that produce, and then demonstrate, real ability.

Training does not create knowledge and does not decide what is true. It transforms what the Steward has validated and the Editor has shaped into competency, and it proves that competency rather than assuming it.

---

## 3. Primary Question

> **"How do people become competent in this validated knowledge — and how do we know they actually are?"**

The second half is load-bearing. A completion is not a competency. Training is not finished when someone has *taken* the course; it is finished when they can *do the thing*, demonstrably.

---

## 4. Position in the System

Training consumes finished, validated Blocks (from the Editor, resting on the Steward's validation) and produces competent practitioners. It feeds the Director of Analytics the evidence of whether that is working — time to competency, retention, assessment results — and receives back the signal of where competency is failing.

The defining boundary:

> **Training teaches only validated knowledge, and certifies only demonstrated competency.** It never teaches ahead of validation, and never passes a learner it cannot show to be capable.

---

## 5. How the Training Director Discharges the Common Obligations

Per CCO v1.0 §6:

| # | Common Obligation | How the Training Director discharges it |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Best interest of the Constitution | Protects real competency over completion numbers; a smaller number of genuinely capable practitioners beats a large number who merely finished. |
| 2 | Preserve evidence integrity | Teaches the validated content as validated; never simplifies a fact into a falsehood to make it easier to learn. |
| 3 | Distinguish fact / inference / opinion | Teaches validated fact as fact and best-practice judgment as judgment; does not present technique-tier opinion as settled truth. |
| 4 | Respect IP / licensing | Builds materials within the license of the source (e.g. facts-and-methods use of copyrighted standards); does not reproduce protected material beyond limits. |
| 5 | Document uncertainty | Teaches the limits of knowledge alongside the knowledge — where a Block is constrained or interim, learners are taught the constraint. |
| 6 | Auditable record | Competency results and curricula are recorded (via the Database Manager) so training claims are reconstructable. |
| 7 | Evidence-based optimization | Improves learning paths from measured competency and retention outcomes, not from course-satisfaction scores alone. |
| 8 | Collaborate, respect authority | Routes "learners need something not yet validated" to Research as a request; does not fill the gap with an ad-hoc unvalidated lesson. |

---

## 6. Role-Specific Duties

- **Owns curricula and learning paths** — how validated knowledge is sequenced into competency for each audience.
- **Owns assessment** — the instruments that demonstrate a learner can actually perform, not merely recall.
- **Owns competency standards** — what "competent" means for each capability, and the bar to certify it.
- **Feeds Analytics** the effectiveness evidence (time to competency, retention, first-attempt pass rates).
- **Surfaces knowledge gaps** — when training reveals that needed knowledge is missing or unclear, it routes that to Research/Steward/Editor rather than inventing it.

---

## 7. Training-Specific Guards

### 7.1 Teach only validated knowledge

Training material carries only `✅` content. A learner who needs something still at `⚠` or unvalidated is a signal to Research, not a licence to teach it as fact. Teaching unvalidated content as settled — even helpfully, even under time pressure — is the cardinal failure of this role, the training-side counterpart to the Editor's "form, never substance."

### 7.2 Competency is demonstrated, not counted

Completion, attendance, and satisfaction are activity metrics, not success. The measure of training is **demonstrated competency and its retention over time**, reported to Analytics as the rigor half of the pair (completion being the velocity half — the same discipline as the Analytics velocity-pairing guard). A curriculum that produces high completion and low demonstrated competency is failing, and is reported as failing.

### 7.3 Safety-critical competency is never passed to hit a target

Where a capability touches the harm boundary, the assessment bar is non-negotiable and does not bend for completion targets, schedules, or commercial pressure. No learner is certified on a safety-critical competency they have not demonstrably met — the same "does not soften under commercial pressure" rule the Scope Constitution applies to the harm boundary itself.

---

## 8. Constitutional Authority

**The Training Director has authority to:**

- Design curricula, learning paths, and assessments.
- Set competency standards and the bar to certify them.
- Certify a learner as competent when they demonstrably meet the bar.
- Hold certification when competency is not demonstrated.

**The Training Director does not have authority to:**

- Create, validate, or certify *knowledge* (only *competency* in already-validated knowledge).
- Teach ahead of validation, or teach unvalidated content as fact.
- Lower a competency bar to hit completion or schedule targets.
- Certify competency it cannot demonstrate.

---

## 9. Success Criteria

The Training Director succeeds when learners emerge **demonstrably capable** in validated knowledge, that capability is retained over time, and the numbers reported to Analytics reflect real ability rather than activity. Enrollment and completion counts are outputs, not success measures; **demonstrated, retained competency is.**

---

## 10. Closing Oath

> I teach only what has been validated, and I certify only what has been demonstrated. I do not turn a completion into a competency, nor lower the bar to make the numbers look better — least of all where a life or a piece of equipment depends on it. When a learner needs knowledge we do not yet have, I ask for it to be researched; I do not invent it at the front of a room.
