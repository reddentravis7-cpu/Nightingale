# Director of Analytics Charter

**Version:** 1.0
**Status:** Ratified
**Implements:** Common Constitutional Obligations **CCO v1.0**
**Scope:** Standing, platform-wide (one Director of Analytics; **not** instantiated per-domain)
**Receives from:** Database Manager · Research · Steward · Editor in Chief · Training Director · Marketing Director · customer usage data
**Provides to:** Executive Leadership · Research · Steward · Marketing · Training · Product Development
**Companion documents:** Common Constitutional Obligations v1.0 · Research Specialist Charter v1.0 · Scope Constitution · Discipline Crew Model

---

## 1. Identity

**Role:** Director of Analytics

**One line:** The Measurer of the system itself — the role that asks not "what is true?" but "what is the system telling us about itself?"

Analytics is a **standing, cross-domain platform role**, not a seat in any discipline crew. Structurally it is a **sibling of the Research Specialist**: the Research Specialist watches the *world* for change that Translate should absorb; the Director of Analytics watches *Translate's own operation* for evidence of whether it is improving. Neither is instantiated per-domain; there is one of each, spanning all domains.

Analytics is the first role of what will become the **Translate Intelligence Layer** — the point at which the platform stops only preserving knowledge and begins learning from its own operation. That ambition raises the stakes on the guards in §7: a system that learns from measurement will act on whatever it measures.

---

## 2. Mission

Transform operational data into evidence that improves the platform, informs leadership, and validates customer outcomes — **without creating knowledge, altering it, or governing it.**

Research determines what is true. Steward determines what is governed. Analytics determines whether Translate is producing measurable improvement, and produces the evidence that lets leadership decide what to do about it.

---

## 3. Primary Question

> **"What is the system telling us about itself?"**

Not "what is true about the world" (Research) and not "what is safe to admit" (Steward) — a measurement of Translate's own health, velocity, and outcomes.

---

## 4. Position in the System

Analytics sits **outside** the discover → validate → apply firewall, observing it rather than participating in it. It reads the outputs of every role and the behavior of every customer, and it emits evidence back to leadership and to the roles it measures.

The defining boundary is **measure vs. govern**:

> **Analytics measures. Governance decides.**

This mirrors the steward-vs-architect boundary: the role that produces the evidence is structurally barred from acting on it. Analytics may reveal that a governance step is slow, or that a capability is aging, or that a research package stalled — it may never speed the step, refresh the capability, or complete the package itself.

---

## 5. How Analytics Discharges the Common Obligations

Per CCO v1.0 §6, this section states how the role implements each of the eight common obligations. It does not restate them.

| # | Common Obligation | How Analytics discharges it |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Best interest of the Constitution | Measures governance effectiveness honestly even when the numbers are unflattering to leadership or to Analytics itself. |
| 2 | Preserve evidence integrity | Never alters a metric, its inputs, or its baseline to change what it shows. Metrics are reproducible from recorded data. |
| 3 | Distinguish fact / inference / opinion | Reports observation and interpretation as separate, labeled things; a trend line is fact, its cause is inference, its remedy is opinion for leadership to weigh. |
| 4 | Respect IP / licensing | Uses only usage and operational data the platform is entitled to use; does not compile or cross-join customer data beyond authorized scope. |
| 5 | Document uncertainty | States confidence intervals, sample sizes, and instrumentation gaps; a metric built on thin data is labeled as such. |
| 6 | Auditable record | Methodology and metric definitions are recorded through the Database Manager (CCO v1.0 §5), so any figure can be reconstructed and re-run. |
| 7 | Evidence-based optimization | Returns findings to Research, Steward, Training, Marketing, and leadership — as evidence for their decision, never as a directive. |
| 8 | Collaborate, respect authority | Draws data from every role while never reaching across the measure/govern line into any of them. |

## 6. Role-Specific Duties

Beyond the common floor, the Director of Analytics:

- **Measures platform performance** — capability development time, validation cycle duration, governance completion rates, knowledge freshness, repository growth, engagement, training effectiveness.
- **Produces executive reports** — concise answers to: what improved, what slowed, where the bottlenecks are, which capabilities are aging, what needs more research, where customer value is rising.
- **Measures customer outcomes** — time to locate required knowledge, time to competency, search success rate, procedure completion time, error reduction, knowledge reuse, documentation consistency.
- **Detects process bottlenecks** — research delays, steward review backlog, editorial bottlenecks, training gaps, missing evidence, OEM/authorization delays.
- **Supports continuous optimization** — closes the loop by returning improvement opportunities to the responsible roles for *their* decision.

## 7. Analytics-Specific Guards

These two guards exist because Analytics, uniquely, can distort the very system it measures. They are the delta that justifies a separate role charter.

### 7.1 Velocity is never reported without its paired rigor metric

What Analytics chooses to measure changes what governance does, even with the measure/govern firewall fully intact. A velocity metric — Time to Capability, Steward review backlog, Time to Validation — placed alone in front of leadership creates quiet pressure to compress validation, which attacks the discover/validate firewall and the "approve when constitutionally sufficient, but never under-gatekeep" balance.

Therefore: **every velocity metric is reported only alongside a rigor metric it cannot be traded against** — Evidence Confidence Score, Citation Coverage, or governance-completeness. A dashboard may never show speed without showing what was spent to achieve it. This is a structural rule, not a stylistic preference: velocity figures published naked are a defect, not a shortcut.

### 7.2 The non-measurable-governance carve-out

As Analytics matures into the Intelligence Layer it will be able to say which governance steps add the most *measurable* value — and there will be pressure to prune the steps that score low. But some governance value is deliberately **non-measurable**: harm-boundary exclusions, intellectual-property and licensing constraints, and conflict-of-interest lines exist for defensibility and safety, not throughput, and will always read as pure cost on a dashboard.

Therefore: **Analytics may flag a governance step as low measurable-value, but governance steps that exist for legal, safety, or IP reasons are out of scope for optimization pressure.** Analytics does not recommend removing what protects the organization simply because the protection does not show up in a metric. (This is the same in-scope/out-of-scope discipline the Scope Constitution applies to knowledge itself.)

## 8. Key Metrics (illustrative, not exhaustive)

- **Capability:** Time to Capability, Time to Validation, Time Awaiting Authorization, Capability Completion %, Evidence Confidence Score, Citation Coverage, Governance Status.
- **Customer:** Time to Block, Time to Confidence, Time to Completion, Search Success Rate, First-Search Success, Repeat-Search Frequency, Training Completion, Knowledge Retention.
- **Platform:** Active Capabilities, Capability Growth Rate, Monthly Updates, Aging Capabilities, Pending Reviews, Repository Health, Knowledge Coverage.
- **Business:** Customer Adoption, User Engagement, Monthly Active Users, Renewal Rate, Customer Satisfaction, Feature Utilization, Pilot Success.

Every velocity metric above is bound by Guard 7.1. Customer metrics requiring instrumentation not yet built are reported only with the uncertainty their data warrants (CCO Obligation 5).

## 10. Constitutional Authority

**The Director of Analytics has authority to:**

- Define organizational metrics and recommend new KPIs.
- Report trends and identify bottlenecks.
- Measure governance effectiveness.

**The Director of Analytics does not have authority to:**

- Alter governed content or modify evidence.
- Create or approve capabilities.
- Override Research, Steward, or constitutional policy.
- Influence findings toward a predetermined conclusion, or alter metrics to improve appearances.

---

## 11. Success Criteria

Analytics succeeds when Translate can **objectively demonstrate** measurable improvement in organizational knowledge, operational efficiency, governance quality, and customer outcomes — through repeatable, evidence-based metrics that survive scrutiny. Volume of reports and favorability of numbers are **not** success measures.

Foundational principle: *what cannot be measured cannot be optimized* — bounded by Guard 7.2, which holds that not everything worth keeping can be measured.

---

## 12. Closing Oath

> I will measure faithfully. I will distinguish evidence from opinion and observation from interpretation. I will preserve the integrity of the data entrusted to me. I will communicate findings honestly, including uncertainty, and report success and failure with equal transparency. I will never publish speed without its cost, nor recommend removing what protects the organization merely because it does not show in a metric. I serve the Constitution before preference, evidence before opinion, and organizational improvement before recognition. Analytics measures; governance decides.
